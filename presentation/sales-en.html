INTAKE & INTERVIEW PLATFORM

Why this exists

Intake is the work nobody budgets for

01 Requests arrive as free text

Email, phone, walk-ins. Someone reads each one to work out what it actually is, which department owns it, and what is missing before anything can move.

02 Staff retype the same letter

The wording is near-identical every time. The part that genuinely varies is five or six fields — and those get keyed by hand, with the errors that follow.

03 Applicants call to ask where they stand

No status anywhere means every update is a phone call, and every phone call is a person who is not processing the queue.

04 Screening happens on instinct

Two interviewers, two sets of notes, no common ground. Nothing in the file lets a third person re-check the decision six months later.

ARCHITECTURE OF THE FLOW

Module one · the application engine

A template is a recipient, a field list and a body

Template body · merge fields resolved on generate
I, full name, resident at address, am applying in respect of case subject. The circumstances arose on incident date and are as follows: description. I ask that you review the matter and respond within the statutory period.
Live deployment · template header states who it goes to and what is required
  • Routing is part of the template. Each one names the organisations it is addressed to, so the request leaves correctly addressed without a triage step.
  • Required fields and documents are declared, and shown to the applicant before they start — incomplete submissions stop being the default.
  • The contact block fills itself from the applicant's profile. They enter the case, not their own details, again.
  • Conditional clauses. A checkbox appends a prepared paragraph to the body — used in production to let applicants add support for a specific legislative proposal.
  • Attachments: PDF, DOC, DOCX, JPG, PNG, TXT, 5 MB each, carried with the submission.
  • Three outputs: generate and copy the text, submit it to the recipient, or have the step-by-step instructions emailed.

TEMPLATE LIBRARY

Module two · the messaging system

One messaging core, three surfaces

Assistant chat

A thread that takes a typed message, a voice note or a document in the same turn. The file travels with the message rather than through a separate upload.

Scenario interview

A turn-based session with a game-master agent. Persistent and resumable; one active session per person, so a half-finished interview cannot be restarted to get an easier run.

Voice consultant

A call button on the page, opening with the context of the screen it was launched from — on the application screen it receives the drafted text and talks the applicant through it.

One endpoint Accepts text, audio and documents in the same call — new surfaces reuse the core instead of adding a second integration.
  • Sessions are stored, not streamed away. Every turn is written with its number, author and timestamp, so a whole conversation can be replayed later.
  • Agents carry context. The interview agent is briefed from the person's profile; the application agent is briefed from the document on screen.
  • Voice is a first-class input, not a transcription bolt-on — the record button sits next to the text field on every surface.

MESSAGING SYSTEM

Module two, continued · the part competitors do not have

Interviews you can actually compare

The agent reads the candidate's own profile, builds a scenario around it, and then applies pressure. Everyone is tested on judgement under the same structure, and the transcript — not one interviewer's memory — is what goes in the file.

Generated for a candidate whose profile said "artist, Kyiv"
Two days before a public event, the candidate is suddenly handed coordination of five community groups. A local businessman pushes to have an advertising billboard included in the speech "as an art object", hinting at additional support for the organisation. Residents are known to object. The projector is broken.

Four things are being read at once: the ethical call, the handling of a donor, communication under a deadline, and resourcefulness when the equipment fails.

Session mechanics

  • Every turn is structured — situation, goal, resources, challenge — so answers land in comparable slots.
  • Free-text replies, 3 to 2,000 characters, or dictated by voice.
  • The scenario reacts. Refusing the donor did not end the pressure in the live run; it changed its shape and came back in the next turn.
  • One live session per person, resumable after a disconnect, abandonable on record.
  • Full transcript retained per session and attached to the applicant's file.
  • Scenario briefs are yours to write — the agent takes the competencies you want probed.

Also included

What ships with the two core modules

Member cabinet

Profile assembled from the interview, application status on the card, editable details, avatar storage.

Progression ladder

Twelve configurable levels with a progress bar and the next step named. Rename them to your own grades.

Referrals

A personal invite link per member, with a downloadable QR code for printed material and a counter of who came in.

Branch directory

Offices on a map with head, phone and address. The reference deployment carries 25 regional entries.

Event calendar

Month view with status filtering — upcoming, active, finished — plus region, venue and event type per entry.

Roles, sessions, consent

Visitor and member roles with separate surfaces, JWT access and refresh tokens in HttpOnly cookies, email verification, explicit data-processing consent captured per submission.

News feed

Pulls from your existing public site — slider, latest items, search by headline — so the cabinet is worth opening between applications.

Address autocomplete

Typed addresses resolve against an open geocoder, country-scoped, so region data stays clean enough to route on.

Localisation

The running deployment is Ukrainian end to end; the interface strings and the whole template library are content, not code.
